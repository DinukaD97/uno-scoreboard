Object

Be the first to play every card in your hand each round. When a round ends, you score points based on the cards left in your opponents' hands. First player to reach 500 points wins the match — this app's Totals and History track that automatically.

What's in the deck

A standard deck has 112 cards: 19 each of blue, green, red, and yellow number cards (0–9), 8 Skip, 8 Reverse and 8 Draw Two cards (two of each color), 4 Wild cards, 4 Wild Draw Four cards, and 4 blank cards for house rules or replacing a lost card.

Setup

Each player draws a card — highest value deals. The dealer shuffles and deals 7 cards to each player, then places the rest facedown as the draw pile. Flip its top card to start the discard pile (if it's a Wild or Wild Draw Four, shuffle it back in and flip a new one).

Taking a turn

Play starts to the dealer's left and passes to the left. On your turn, play a card that matches the top of the discard pile by color, number, or symbol — or play a Wild card. Can't go? Draw one card from the draw pile; play it if it fits, otherwise your turn ends. If the draw pile ever runs out, reshuffle the discard pile (keeping its top card in place) and keep playing. You may also choose not to play a card you could legally play (reneging) — draw instead; you may play that drawn card if it fits, but you can no longer play a card from your hand that turn.

Calling "UNO" & going out

When you play your second-to-last card, say "UNO" out loud. Forget, and you can still "catch" yourself safely — but only until another player catches you first, or the next player begins their turn (draws or plays). Caught too late? You draw 4 penalty cards. A hand ends the instant a player plays their last card; if the last card played is a Draw Two or Wild Draw Four, the next player still draws those cards.

Scoring a round

Add up every card left in your opponents' hands — that total becomes the round winner's score, entered as a round in the Current Match tab. (Blank cards have no fixed value — agree on one with your house rule, or match whatever card they're replacing.)
This is the standard scoring method, and the one this app follows: the round winner's total climbs, and highest score at 500 wins. The official rules also describe an alternate method where every player tracks their own leftover-card total instead, and when someone hits 500 the player with the lowest total wins — see Challenge UNO below if you'd rather play that way.

Winning

Keep playing rounds until someone's running total reaches 500. Use Pick Dealer at the start of a match to randomly choose who deals (and plays) first.

Blank cards

Use a blank card to replace a card that's lost or damaged from your physical deck (just write its value on it), or invent your own house rule and write it on a blank card to shuffle into the deck.

Variants

Official alternate ways to play — this app's scoring always follows the standard rules above regardless of which variant you use at the table.
Two players — Reverse and Skip both let you take another turn immediately instead of passing play. A Draw Two or Wild Draw Four you play sends the draw back to your one opponent, then play returns straight to you.
Partners (4 players) — Sit across from your partner and keep your hands secret from each other. The instant either partner goes out, the hand ends; add together the cards left in both opponents' hands for your team's score.
Multi-table tournaments — With 8 players, run two 4-player partner tables at once, each player paired with someone at the other table, and total every player's scores across all hands.
Challenge UNO — Instead of scoring the winner each hand, keep a running total of the points each player gets caught holding. Reaching a set target (500 suggested) eliminates that player. When two remain, they finish head-to-head using the two-player rules above; whoever hits the target first loses, and the other is the winner.
